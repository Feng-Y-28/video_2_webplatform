# Short-video structure for explainers

Distilled from creator advice and retention breakdowns widely shared on
short-video platforms, plus what held up in practice. Treat the numbers as
rules of thumb, not laws.

## The five-part skeleton (30–60 s)

| Time | Part | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | Hook | Say the thing that "shouldn't be true". Must work on mute. |
| 3–8 s | Target | Name the viewer's assumption ("you'd think it's the multipliers…"); promise the answer. |
| 8–25 s | Evidence | Concrete numbers and a visible mechanism — proof, not opinion. |
| 25–45 s | Turn | Flip the first half or reveal the cost. Often lands around 2/3 of runtime. |
| End | Line | One sentence worth a screenshot. |

Where viewers leave, in order of size: when the explaining starts (~8–12 s),
in the first 3 s, when 5 s pass with nothing new, and when the conclusion
comes too early. Long runtime is not the problem; slow stretches are.

## Hooks (≤ 23 characters so it is not truncated)

- **Denial** — "Stop doing X; it isn't for you." Lowest risk; you must then give the alternative.
- **Number** — "8 cores. Only 2 doing work." The number must be sourced.
- **Confession** — "This took four tries; three failed for the same reason." Highest trust.

## Knowledge-chain suspense

Use a familiar object to lead the viewer to an unfamiliar answer. Each next
question should grow out of the last answer. That way the suspense comes from
the content and does not need to be added as a slogan. If you cannot answer
the next question with a source, narrow the topic; do not make up a plausible
answer.

## Common failures

1. Copying the structure but not the information density (five numbers vs "this is important").
2. Making the counter-intuitive claim false or exaggerated. Viewers find out by about second 9.
3. Putting the highlight past the point where most viewers are gone.
4. Squeezing four minutes of material into 40 s. That is breathless, not dense.
5. Treating platforms as one. Some reward story, some fast information, some
   strong visuals and subtitles.

## Production habit

Write the ending first. Then make the first 10 s and review it before you
make the rest.
